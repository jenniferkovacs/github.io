## About Me

I am an  evolutionary ecologist whose work sits at the intersection of symbiosis, genomic change, and environmental transformation. I am broadly trained in behavioral and quantitative genetics, and my research asks how organisms and communities respond to rapid ecological change across scales— from microbial interactions to urban ecosystems.

My research integrates open data sources such as eBird, NEON, and GBIF with reproducible, data-intensive workflows to examine how processes like species interactions, environmental stress, and the long-term legacy of structural inequities—including redlining—shape ecological communities. I am especially interested in how urban systems can both constrain and support biodiversity in unexpected ways.

At Agnes Scott College, I lead a collaborative, student-centered research program where undergraduates engage as scientists and co-creators of knowledge. My students contribute to conference presentations, data-driven projects, and peer-reviewed publications, and much of my work is grounded in expanding access to quantitative, computational, and open science approaches in biology.

More broadly, I am committed to rethinking how we teach biology in a data-rich world, integrating tools from data science, AI, and open science into undergraduate education. Programs like ESIIL are an exciting space for me to continue growing this work.

Outside of my academic life, I am usually in the woods with my twin boys, foraging for mushrooms, or experimenting in my garden.

## What I'm Excited to Learn in Earth Data Science

I am excited to build fluency in Python and open Earth data workflows so I can bring these tools directly into my research and my students' hands. My lab already uses R and GIS to analyze biodiversity and citizen science data, and I want to expand that toolkit to work more fluidly with remote sensing products, spatial data at large scales, and reproducible, shareable workflows rooted in open science principles. I'm especially excited to connect environmental data science to the environmental justice questions my lab is already asking, and to bring what I learn back to Agnes Scott students so we can continue these conversations.

## Questions I Want to Answer Using Earth Data Science

- How does the spatial legacy of redlining shape present-day urban bird biodiversity, and does that pattern hold across taxonomic groups beyond birds?
- At what spatial and temporal scales does urbanization homogenize biological communities, and are there landscape conditions that support unexpected urban biodiversity?
- How do landscape connectivity and habitat fragmentation influence the distribution of arthropod communities in rapidly urbanizing areas like metro Atlanta?
- How can open Earth data tools and workflows be integrated into authentic undergraduate research experiences that are accessible to students from all backgrounds and institutions?
